## PART 2 — THE SYSTEMATIC BIAS, found

I re-derived every dossier's rubric score arithmetically and compared it to the number I actually published on the board. For 16 of 27 they differ by more than a full point. **The published board number is not the output of the rubric — it is judgment layered on top of it.** That is not automatically wrong, but the residual should be random. It is not.

**Where the board runs ABOVE the arithmetic:** Brock Alpher (+1.6), Ansh Barmecha (+1.5), Matthew Ong (+1.4), David Chen (+1.2), Anvesha Gupta (+1.0), Will Marra (+1.0).
**Where the board runs BELOW it:** Devi Gupta (−1.1), Ziad El Hout (−0.8), Selim Saab (−0.8), Laura Lu (−0.7), Cade Collins (−0.7).

The line separating those two groups is not drive, and it is not strength.

> ### It is findability.
> Everyone in the top group has something **I could open with my own eyes** — a live URL, a city permit, a prize amount, a revenue figure. Everyone in the bottom group is someone whose claims I could not corroborate and whose file I marked *"absent from his own LinkedIn."*

**I have been scoring the quality of the evidence and reporting it as the quality of the person.** Absence of a footprint is not evidence of weakness. Plenty of real work — service jobs, family businesses, running a team, facilitating a room — never gets posted anywhere. Aaron Wu is a victim of this in one direction (teens and conversations leave no trace) and a beneficiary in the other (his posting is maximally findable).

### The fix: strength and confidence are two different numbers
I am not going to blind-raise the unverifiable people — Ryan's own standard is *never guess and present it as fact*, and unverified still means he cannot rely on it in the room. The correct fix is to say plainly which scores are **floors with a wide error bar**, because that tells Ryan **which calls are worth making first.**

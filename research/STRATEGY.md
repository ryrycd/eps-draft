## 1. Should you pass a round? **No. Not one.**

Your prompt leaned toward "maybe," on the logic that under $1M separated past winners and a R1 pass plus the under-four bonus is exactly $1M. I think that reasoning is wrong, for four reasons.

**a) The pool is short, so a pass may not be refillable.** 43 players (possibly 44 — I count 55 students, Len says 56), 48 slots. And the draft-process doc names 3 HR + 3 IT Officers as separate roles; if those six come out of the pool it is 37–38 for 48. The rules say if you pass you "may select as many students as needed at the end to have 4 players" — but that assumes players remain. With 43 players for 48 slots, five slots go unfilled — spread across somewhere between two and five CEOs depending on who passes. Passing volunteers you into that group while *also* giving up the pick.

**b) You are comparing a one-time payment to a 13-week compounding contributor.** $600K arrives once. A fourth teammate shows up to fourteen sessions, carries a share of every weekly challenge, takes a book summary off your plate, and adds a body to "feed 50+ people and you may not pay for any of it." To reach $20M you have to *win challenges*, and challenges are won with hands and hours.

**c) The $1M-spread statistic is being read backwards.** Less than $1M separated the *top* teams — teams that had all already executed well all semester. The spread at the top is small precisely because those teams were all fully staffed and firing. Cash does not put you in that group; performance does. Buying $1M of margin while giving up 25% of your labour is trading the thing that gets you to the top for a tiebreaker you only need once you are there.

**d) You personally cannot afford fewer hands.** Your own input sheet says systems are your ceiling and you generate results faster than you organise them. A three-player team means more coordination load on the person who is worst at coordination load. That is the specific failure mode to avoid.

**The only pass I would even entertain** is Round 4 at $100K if — and only if — every name on your board is gone and the remaining pool is genuinely dead. Given twenty invisible players, that will not happen. **Plan to draft four. Do not pass.**

## 2. THE RULES OF THE GAME — READ CAREFULLY

### Structure
- 56 students in the class. 12 are CEOs (competitors of mine). The rest (~44) are the draftable player pool.
- There are also **3 HR Officers and 3 IT Officers** — confirm whether these are drawn from the player pool or are separate roles, and flag the answer.
- Each CEO drafts a team of **4 players** over **4 rounds**.
- **Snake order**: whoever picks 1st in Round 1 picks last in Round 2, and so on.
- The draft is a **single live event** at the first class. Trades have a **2-minute time limit**, which confirms it is live and fast.

### Money
- Every team starts with **$1,000,000** in venture capital. This is the running scoreboard for the semester; teams gain and lose it through weekly challenges.
- A CEO may **pass** on their pick and take cash instead: **$600K (R1), $500K (R2), $250K (R3), $100K (R4)**.
- If you take fewer than 4 players in the initial 4 rounds, you get an **additional $400K** and select from the "hidden gems" after two additional rounds.
- If you passed, you refill to 4 players at the end from the remaining pool. **Passing costs talent quality, not team size.**
- Historically, winning teams reached **$20,000,000**, and **less than $1,000,000 separated the top teams**. A single R1 pass plus the under-four bonus is $1M — the entire spread between top finishers.

### The refusal rule
- A drafted player may **refuse to join, one time only, in the entire draft**.
- If refused, the drafting CEO receives the cash equivalent of that round, and the player is **forced onto the next team that selects them**.
- Therefore: **a refusal pays exactly what a pass pays but is strictly worse** — it burns the turn, hands the target to a rival, and signals a misread. Pre-commitments exist to eliminate this risk entirely.

### Other mechanics
- CEOs may **trade with each other** and may **spend capital** to buy a better draft position or acquire a player from another team.
- Firing a player or losing one to a drop carries **no monetary penalty**.
- Students joining the class after the draft pick a team or are assigned to a team that lost a player.
- **Diversification is scored.** Len awards extra points based on different majors, genders, countries of origin, religions. He explicitly warns that drafting friends may hurt your chances.
- Len's instruction to CEOs: use out-of-the-box thinking to learn about fellow students, contact them before the first class to ask if they'd join if drafted, and recruit non-credit students (with his approval on terms).
- Len's stated value: **"rat-like cunning"** is an explicit grading criterion.

### The class itself (this determines what kind of teammate actually wins)
- Meets **Wednesdays 3:00–6:00 PM, Olin 120**, and often runs to 6:30 or 7:00.
- **65% of my grade is Total Team Score.** Plus 5% team bonus points for out-of-the-box thinking.
- **Laptops and phones are banned in class.** Penalties are severe. Everything I bring to draft day must be **on paper**.
- Weekly challenges are real-world hustle tasks, not case write-ups. Documented past examples: feed the entire class (50–100 people) without being allowed to pay for any of it; pitch a venture capital firm for funding; raise money for the Jimmy Fund Walk; turn an ordinary object into a business.
- Each week a designated team member **presents a book summary from memory** — reading off a page is not allowed.
- Grading rewards creative and original thinking, depth of analysis, defending positions, and effort sustained across the semester.

**Implication you must internalize:** the winning teammate profile is not the most decorated résumé. It is the person who can be handed an absurd task with no budget and come back having done it. Weight your evaluation accordingly.

### The evidence standard — this is non-negotiable

Every factual claim in a dossier must be tagged:

- **[VERIFIED]** — I found it at a specific public source. Include the URL.
- **[STATED]** — the person claimed it in their brag sheet or résumé. Not independently confirmed.
- **[INFERRED]** — my reasoning from evidence. Show the reasoning explicitly.
- **[UNKNOWN]** — I could not determine this.

**Never guess and present it as fact.** GPA, club membership, and internal accomplishments are frequently not public. If you cannot find something, write [UNKNOWN]. A fabricated credential that promotes someone into my top tier is far more damaging than an honest gap. If your confidence in a claim is below high, say so in the line itself.

## 5. THE SCORING RUBRIC

Score each person 1–10 on each dimension, with the evidence cited underneath. Weights, in my priority order:

| Dimension | Weight | What I actually mean |
|---|---|---|
| **Independent drive & follow-through** | 40% | If I assign this person a task, does it come back done, and done well, without me chasing them? |
| **Raw capability & skill depth** | 25% | Genuine ability — technical, creative, analytical, commercial. |
| **Complementarity to me** | 20% | Systems and organization, structure-building, ideation and whiteboarding, design/visual, presenting. |
| **Diversification value** | 15% | Different major, gender, country of origin, background from mine and from the rest of my likely team. Len scores this. |

### Signals of genuine independent drive — hunt for these
- Multi-year sustained commitment to one thing; promotion or growth **within** an organization
- Things that **shipped and still exist**: registered entities, live sites, real paying customers, published work
- Quantified outcomes clearly **in their own scope**, not their employer's
- Unglamorous work done well: operations, logistics, back office, family business grind
- Evidence of doing work nobody assigned them
- Building something under a real constraint (no budget, no permission, no support)

### Anti-signals — name these explicitly when you see them
- Titles with no verifiable artifact behind them
- "Stealth" or unnamed projects with nothing checkable
- Breadth without depth: many one-semester affiliations, nothing held long
- Prestige-brand résumés where every accomplishment belongs to the employer
- Self-disclosed procrastination, perfectionism/analysis paralysis, or "I move too fast for my team" — several people volunteered these about themselves; treat them as free intelligence and weigh them honestly
- Overcommitment: if their fall is already stacked, their bandwidth for a 65%-of-grade team is limited

Be blunt. I want honest assessments, not diplomatic ones. If someone's brag sheet is the most impressive in the class and I should still pass on them, say that and say why.

## 6. THE TWO-AXIS BOARD

Produce **two independent scores** per person:

- **DESIRE (1–10)** — how much I want them, per the rubric above.
- **SCARCITY (1–10)** — how likely another CEO takes them early. Judge this from how their brag sheet *reads to a casual skimmer*, brand names on the résumé, obvious credentials, and how visible they are.

Priority is **desire × scarcity**, not desire alone. A 9-desire person nobody else has noticed can wait until Round 3. An 8-desire person every CEO will chase must be my Round 1.

Explicitly identify **hidden gems**: high desire, low scarcity. These are where the draft is won. Also identify **traps**: high scarcity, lower true desire — people who will go early on reputation and shouldn't be my pick.

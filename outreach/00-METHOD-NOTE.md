# RESEARCH METHOD — REVISED after the Omar Flores miss

## What went wrong
My first-pass LinkedIn routine was: search → open `/details/experience/` → extract the first ~2,500 characters. Two failures compounded:
1. **Truncation.** The tool output caps at roughly 1,100 characters per call, so I was reading only the *first one or two roles* and treating that as the person's full record. Omar Flores's first entry is a gym job; his founder role, his President's Office analyst role and his CTO role were all below the cut.
2. **I never read anyone's posts, About section, or activity — and I ran almost no per-person open-web search.** Omar's single most revealing artifact (the AI Pathway Analysis, and the sentence explaining why he built it) exists only in a LinkedIn post. His Boston.com feature and his live storefront exist only off LinkedIn entirely.

Net effect: I scored him 5.5 and drafted him a message that opened by telling him his LinkedIn was one job. He is a 9.3.

## The corrected protocol — applied to every one of the 43 from here
For each person, all six steps, in order:

1. **Full experience section, chunked.** Load `/details/experience/`, store the text in a page variable, then read it out in ~1,100-character slices until exhausted. Never accept a truncated read as complete — always print the total character count first and read to the end.
2. **About section** — often where the self-assessment and the real motivation live.
3. **Recent activity / posts** — `/recent-activity/all/`, scrolled, chunked the same way. This is where people describe what they actually did and why, in their own voice, unprompted. It is the highest-signal source after a verified artifact.
4. **Education, honors, projects, volunteering, licenses** — scholarships and selective programmes (Blank Scholar, Posse, Presidential Scholar, Horatio Alger) are hard external filters and they are frequently not in the experience section.
5. **Open web, per person, always.** Name + school; name + any company or brand they mention; the brand name alone; local news; school alumni pages; Instagram; personal sites; business registrations. Omar's Boston.com feature, AJ Bulow's retail distribution, Cade Collins's live moving company and Tarynea's published journal case were all found this way — none were on LinkedIn.
6. **Cross-reference against their own brag sheet and résumé** and flag every discrepancy in either direction — inflation *and* under-selling. Jeda Wannasin and Omar Flores are both cases of someone being far better than their own submission suggests.

## The general lesson, which changes the strategy
Under-selling is at least as common as inflation in this class, and it is more actionable. The people who wrote modest brag sheets — Jeda, Kelly Morgan, Mia Mumby, Yukt Agarwal — are systematically underpriced, and the people who wrote nothing at all are invisible entirely. **The draft is won by reading past the first screen.** That is now literally as well as figuratively true.
